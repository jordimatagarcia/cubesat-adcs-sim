# Diagnosis of the thesis-variant ADCS

What limits the reference ADCS (variant `thesis`), measured rather than guessed. This page
interprets the numbers in [diagnostics.md](diagnostics.md) (generated by
`tools/diagnose.py`) and the experiments in `tools/diagnosis_experiments.py`. It changes
nothing in the algorithm; it decides what the improved variant has to change.

## Method

- **Consistency.** For each part of the estimate (attitude, rate, both biases), the error
  against the truth is divided by the filter's own σ. A consistent filter has about 99.7% of
  these normalised errors within ±3 and a mean square near 1. Far above 1: the filter is
  overconfident (it believes it is more accurate than it is).
- **Innovations.** The normalised innovation squared (NIS) of each measurement group;
  expected value 4 (q), 3 (sun), 3 (B), 3 (ω).
- **FLAE vs filter.** The attitude error of the FLAE measurement itself next to the
  filter's, to tell measurement problems from filter problems.
- **Scenarios.** Thesis cases 1a, 2b and 4, plus two with realistic sensors (datasheet-level
  noise and a constant gyro bias): `realistic_1a` (case 1a over two orbits) and `detumble`.
- **Experiments.** Each hypothesis tested by changing exactly one thing.

## Findings

### 1. The attitude is only observable through FLAE

The measurement model is `h(x) = x`: the magnetometer row is `y_B = B + b_mag` with `B` its
own state, so the magnetometer never involves the attitude q. The only measurement that
constrains q is the FLAE output.

In eclipse FLAE is fed the filter's own propagated sun vector. That pseudo-measurement looks
circular, but it is what keeps the estimate bounded: disabling it (experiment H1) turns a
0.4° median eclipse error into 57°, with the filter diverging in every eclipse:

| Case, 3000 s | Eclipse error, median / p95 / max (°) |
|---|---|
| case_1a, as is | 0.39 / 2.70 / 3.77 |
| case_1a, no q measurement in eclipse | 57.4 / 167 / 175 |
| realistic_1a, as is | 0.37 / 1.54 / 2.44 |
| realistic_1a, no q measurement in eclipse | 58.1 / 168 / 176 |

It works because FLAE combines the real magnetometer reading with the filtered sun vector,
so two of the three rotation axes do get real information. But the filter treats the result
as an independent full-attitude measurement with σ ≈ 0.6°, so it is badly **overconfident in
eclipse**: the attitude's mean z² is 11 (case 1a) and 59 (realistic_1a), only 67–83% of
errors stay inside 3σ, and the 3σ bound stays flat at ~0.8° while the real error climbs to
2–9° (figure `realistic_1a`). An overconfident filter rejects the corrections that arrive at
sunrise and cannot tell good estimates from bad ones.

**Cause:** the measurement model, not the tuning. **Implication:** the improved filter
should measure the vectors themselves, `y_sun = A(q) s_i` and `y_B = A(q) B_i + b_mag`, so
the magnetometer constrains the attitude directly (two axes in eclipse; the third becomes
observable as the field direction turns along the orbit) and no measurement is counted twice.

### 2. The magnetometer bias is frozen by the tuning and unobservable by construction

Case 2b: the bias estimate's σ is about 1e-4 µT against a true bias of 30 µT (mean z² of
2e9). The thesis tuning (P_b0 = 1e-12 µT², W_b = 1e-11 µT² per step) makes the filter
certain the bias is zero.

Loosening that tuning (experiment H4: P_b0 = 100 µT², W_b = 1e-4) is not enough: the
estimate reaches −9.7 of −30 µT and the attitude still reaches 53°. With `y_B = B + b_mag`
the filter can only ever see the sum; it separates the two only transiently, when the bias
appears faster than the field state can follow. And FLAE keeps feeding the raw, biased
field into the attitude measurement, exactly as the thesis concluded (§5.6.4).

**Implication:** the same as finding 1. With `y_B = A(q) B_i + b_mag` and the attitude
pinned by the sun sensor, the bias is the difference between the measured and the predicted
field, which is observable.

### 3. The gyro bias acts as a noise sink

The bias filter's gain on the gyro rows is 0.82: each step, 82% of the gyro innovation goes
into the "bias". In realistic_1a the bias estimate scatters by 0.026 °/s from one step to
the next, nearly the gyro noise itself (0.03 °/s), while its average is right (0.0501 vs
0.05 °/s true). The cause is W_b = 1e-6 (rad/s)² per 0.1 s step, which lets the bias move
0.06 °/s per step.

The thesis needed that: its test biases are 5 °/s steps that appear and vanish within
seconds (cases 2a, 3b). A real gyro bias drifts slowly. In sunlight the noise sink is
harmless because the attitude measurement corrects everything; in eclipse it lets the
bias, and with it the integrated attitude, wander.

**Implication:** model the bias as a slow random walk (W_b several orders of magnitude
smaller) and handle sudden faults separately: detect them from the innovations (NIS) and
then temporarily inflate the bias covariance.

### 4. Case 4: measurement noise mismatched

In case 4 the measurement noise V was left at the nominal values while the sensors became
much noisier (the thesis did the same on purpose). The innovations show it: NIS 34 for q
(expected 4) and 12 for ω (expected 3), and the attitude is overconfident (mean z² 6.5 in
sunlight). With V matched to the sensors (experiment H3) the sunlit error improves from
0.62° to 0.38° median (worst 2.3° → 1.6°), but the eclipse error grows (1.2° → 2.4° median),
because the eclipse estimate depends on the overweighted pseudo-measurement of finding 1.

**Cause:** tuning, made worse by finding 1. Matching V is right but only pays off fully
with a measurement model that doesn't depend on that pseudo-measurement.

### 5. Detumble divergence: eclipse coasting at high rate

The detumble scenario diverges in eclipse (122° p95). Experiments separate the suspects:

| Detumble, first 3000 s | Eclipse error, median / p95 / max (°) | Body rate in eclipse |
|---|---|---|
| as is (control on, 45° sun FOV) | 8.0 / 122 / 135 | 1.6 °/s |
| control off | 3.1 / 70.8 / 115 | 9.8 °/s |
| full-sphere sun coverage | 7.9 / 120 / 132 | 1.6 °/s |
| control off + full coverage | 2.9 / 68.3 / 111 | 9.8 °/s |

Sun-sensor coverage gaps make no difference. Control makes it worse but is not the cause:
without it the estimate still diverges. What remains is findings 1 and 3 together: in
eclipse the attitude about the field direction rests on gyro integration, the noisy bias
estimate corrupts the rate, and the overconfident covariance doesn't let the filter
recover. The rate and bias are overconfident even in sunlight here (mean z² 26 and 10).

### 6. Simulator: the duty cycle has no effect at 10 Hz

Experiment H2 gave bit-identical results for 80% and 100% duty cycle. With the ADCS at
10 Hz and the dynamics also at 10 Hz there is one dynamics step per control period, and the
coils are on at its start, so the duty cycle never switches them off. Scenarios that use
the duty cycle need a finer dynamics step (e.g. 0.02 s).

### 7. The thesis cases flatter the filter

With the thesis's near-ideal sensors (case 1a) the filter is strongly *underconfident* in
sunlight (mean z² ≈ 0): its covariance reflects the tuning, not the sensors. Results with
those sensors say little about real hardware, which is why `realistic_1a` exists.

## Proposed changes for the improved variant (steps 2–4)

| Problem | Change | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|
| q observable only through FLAE (1), magnetometer bias (2) | Vector measurements `y_sun = A(q) s_i`, `y_B = A(q) B_i + b_mag`; FLAE only to initialise | Magnetometer constrains the attitude in eclipse; magnetometer bias observable; no double counting |
| Quaternion as 4 additive UKF states (thesis §5.7) | Error-state attitude: 3 rotation parameters around a reference quaternion (USQUE, Crassidis & Markley 2003) | Consistent attitude covariance; the state shrinks from 13 + 6 to 3 + 3 + 3 (attitude, gyro bias, magnetometer bias), with the gyro as the rate input |
| Gyro bias noise sink (3) | Small random-walk W_b; NIS-based fault detection inflates P_b on a bias step | Clean bias estimate in normal operation, fast response to the thesis's step faults |
| Mismatched V (4) | V from the sensor models, documented | Consistent innovations |
| Duty cycle no-op (6) | Finer dynamics step where duty cycling is used | The simulator models the coils as configured |

A multiplicative EKF on the same model serves as the industry-standard baseline. All of
this lands as variant `improved`; variant `thesis` stays frozen with its golden vectors and
results.
